Who we are

Our Story

Established in 2013, the Australian Asian Cultural Association (AACA) was founded to assist newly arrived migrants and families from Asian cultural backgrounds as they settled into life in Australia.

Over more than a decade, AACA has evolved from providing community information sessions and settlement support into a broader community organisation delivering educational, cultural and family-focused programs. Through partnerships with community organisations and service providers, AACA has helped individuals and families access information, resources and opportunities that improve their quality of life.

One of AACA's most enduring initiatives is the Chinese Seniors Association. First formed in 2008, the group joined AACA in 2016 and continues to bring older community members together through regular social, cultural and wellbeing activities.

Today, AACA continues to support families through community engagement, cultural celebrations, educational programs and volunteer participation. Looking ahead, the organisation is committed to expanding opportunities in language education, cultural learning and community development that strengthen family and community wellbeing.

What guides us

Mission & Vision

Our Mission

To support individuals and families from Asian cultural backgrounds by fostering community connections, promoting lifelong learning, encouraging cultural participation, and enhancing family wellbeing.

Through community programs, educational initiatives, cultural activities and social engagement opportunities, AACA strives to create a welcoming and inclusive environment where people can connect, participate and thrive.

Our Vision

To build connected families, vibrant communities and a brighter future for current and future generations.

We envision a community where people from diverse Asian cultural backgrounds feel welcomed, supported and empowered to contribute meaningfully to Australian society while maintaining pride in their cultural heritage.

Since 2013

Our History

  1. 2008

    The Chinese Seniors Association is formed as a community group for older community members, meeting at the old Hornsby PCYC site.

  2. 2013

    The Australian Asian Cultural Association (AACA) is established.

  3. 2016

    The Chinese Seniors Association joins AACA, and AACA moves to its current home in Hornsby.

  4. 2016 – 2023

    AACA presents a Mandarin-language program on community radio station Triple H FM 100.1, produced by a dedicated team of volunteers.

Thank you to our radio volunteers

From 2016 to 2023, AACA’s Mandarin program on Triple H FM 100.1 was made possible by a dedicated team of volunteers – eighteen people when the program began, with many more contributing over the years. Some have since moved on to new chapters in their lives, but their contribution to our community lives on. We sincerely thank every one of them, named and unnamed.

– Diana Hu, Program Coordinator

Governance

Our Team

AACA is run entirely by volunteers – no one is paid. As an incorporated association, AACA is governed by an elected committee. Our office bearers each hold a defined role and carry out the responsibilities of that role under our constitution.

In practice, we work as one team. Because we are all volunteers, we support each other and step in to back one another up whenever it is needed.

The committee is elected each year by our members at the Annual General Meeting. Anyone who supports our objects can become a member.

To contact our team, please email admin@aacansw.org.au.

How we are governed

Constitution

AACA is an incorporated association registered with NSW Fair Trading under the Associations Incorporation Act 2009 (NSW).

AACA has adopted the NSW Fair Trading Model Constitution without changes.

On 5 August 2026, our members adopted a new constitution by special resolution at a general meeting. It has been lodged with NSW Fair Trading and takes effect once it is registered. Until then, the Model Constitution continues to apply.
